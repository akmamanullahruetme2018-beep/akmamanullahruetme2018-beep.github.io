PROJECT 01 / WORM GEARBOX3RD YEAR · 2ND SEMESTER

Understanding mechanisms
by building them.

A worm gearbox pairs a screw-like worm with a toothed wheel to transmit rotational motion through intersecting axes. For our undergraduate project, the practical challenge was to translate mechanism concepts into components that could be fabricated, handled and assembled.

The images show a 3D-printing workspace, component handling, worm-and-wheel elements and a housing. They document the prototype development experience; they are not evidence of a measured load rating, efficiency, reduction ratio or formal endurance test.

THE PROCESSFROM ACADEMIC DESIGN TO OBJECT
01

Study the mechanism

Consider how a worm, gear wheel, shafts and casing interact; identify fit and alignment considerations.

02

Produce components

Use a desktop 3D-printing environment to produce physical prototype parts and inspect features.

03

Assemble and fit

Handle the printed worm, wheel and associated components, checking their physical relationship.

04

Document & reflect

Photograph stages of the build and identify areas needing tolerances, materials and test data for rigorous evaluation.

THE LEARNINGENGINEERING JUDGMENT

Not every lesson
can be simulated.

Design has to meet materials.

Real components expose tolerances, layer finish, stiffness and assembly considerations that idealized diagrams can hide.

Communication is part of engineering.

A physical prototype makes complex kinematics easier to explain and invites critical questions about manufacturability.

Evidence has boundaries.

No verified gear ratio, torque rating, test protocol or measured efficiency was supplied with the project. This case study therefore focuses on fabrication and assembly, not unmeasured performance claims.
